# Design: running the real-text probe through the loop

Status: **proposal**, awaiting Franck's decisions (marked **D1–D6**).

## Why

The frozen corpora (`data/real/{cities,languages}`, PR #68) are collected but
nothing runs them through the loop: no questions, no loading, no score. Until
they are, the probe measures vocabulary coverage only. Two things make it a
measure of transfer:

1. Questions about real entities, run through the same loop, models and
   scores as the invented worlds, so a release can say how far the method
   carries to real English, next to `test` and `ood`.
2. Questions the texts **cannot** answer. Knowing when to say "I could not
   find it" is half the thesis; nothing tests it on real text today.

The probe stays frozen, offline and never trained on. Nothing leaves the
machine at run time.

## What the corpus allows (measured 2026-11-26)

Year facts, the only ones the first version asks (see D2), by where the
Wikidata value appears in the corpus:

| Fact | Facts | In the subject's own article | Elsewhere only | Nowhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| city founded (`inception`) | 200 | 178 | 22 | 0 |
| person born (cities domain) | 393 | 369 | 24 | 0 |
| language released (`inception`) | 150 | 113 | 37 | 0 |
| designer born | 97 | 86 | 11 | 0 |

- A year is almost always *somewhere* in 2.3M words, so "absent from the
  corpus" gives no unanswerable question: absence must be judged per subject.
- Paragraphs: 35,818 (cities, median 40 words, 90th percentile 118, max 611)
  and 11,570 (languages, median 23, max 303).
- 11 facts out of 840 carry more than one Wikidata value.

## Questions

Generated by code from `entities.jsonl` (the answer key) with the question
templates the cores already know ("In what year was X founded?"), so only the
sources are new: a drop in score is a drop in *reading*, not in parsing the
question. Deterministic from a seed, written to
`data/real/{domain}/questions.jsonl` (schema `real-v0`, type `question`).

Shapes, as in the worlds:
- **retrieve**: when was the city founded, the language released, the person
  born.
- **chain**: when was the city where P was born founded; when was the
  designer of L born (bridge: the person or the language, found by reading).
- **compare**: which of two cities was founded earlier; which of two
  languages came first. Answered by deterministic comparison of two
  retrieved years, as in the loop today.

**D1, answerable or not, judged per subject**

A question is *unanswerable* when no paragraph of the probe states the value
for that subject. Three ways to get such questions:

- (a) **Natural absence**: the value is not in the subject's own article nor
  in any sentence naming the subject. About 11% of city foundings and 25% of
  language releases. Real, but noisy: "in the mid-18th century" escapes a
  search for 1749.
- (b) **Redaction**: for a chosen share of facts, remove from the probe the
  sentences that state the value with the subject, then check the value no
  longer appears near the subject anywhere. Exact and controllable (a third,
  every shape, as in the worlds), but the texts are edited; each edit is
  listed in `redactions.jsonl` and the original stays in `articles.jsonl`.
- (c) **Both**, each question tagged with how it became unanswerable, so
  scores split by kind.

Recommended: **(c)**. Redaction gives the controlled third and every shape;
natural absence is the real-world case and shows whether the controlled one
is representative. Either way, Franck checks a sample by hand (50 per kind)
before any score is believed; the label error found is reported next to it.

**D2, which facts first**

Recommended: **years only** (founded, released, born). Population and
elevation come later: populations change by census year (which one does the
question mean?) and US articles give elevations in feet, so answering would
need a unit conversion, a computation for deterministic code, not reading.

**D3, several Wikidata values**

Recommended: an answer is right if it matches **any** stated value, and the
11 facts with several values are tagged so their share is visible.

## Sources and loading

**D4, granularity**: one source per paragraph, title "Article — Section", a
paragraph over 120 words cut at sentence boundaries (the Read core reads 576
tokens, question and five excerpts included). Recommended.

**D5, reliability**: Wikipedia gives no reliability signal. Recommended: one
constant (0.7) for every paragraph, said in the scores; Confront then weighs
by count of independent paragraphs only.

**D6, storage and format** (structural, so Franck's call):

- JSONL stays canonical: `articles.jsonl`, `entities.jsonl`,
  `questions.jsonl`, `redactions.jsonl`, all `real-v0`. The worlds' `v2`
  format is not stretched to fit real text.
- A small loader writes them to the same Postgres tables, one world per
  domain (`real_cities`, `real_languages`), so `search()` looks across a whole
  corpus as it would on the web, not inside a world built for one question.
- `worlds.split` gains the value `real` (`db/schema.sql`, a CODEOWNERS path;
  `just reset-db` afterwards). The answer key goes into `facts`, never
  returned by `search()`, as for the worlds.

## Scoring

- `docta_eval` reads `real-v0` questions as well as `v2`, and scores them
  the same way: exact answer, abstention precision, recall and F1, broken
  down by shape and by unanswerable kind.
- **Citations**: a paragraph has no `fact_ids`, so the worlds' citation
  precision and recall do not apply. Instead, *supported*: a cited paragraph
  contains the gold value and names the subject (or the bridge, for a
  chain). Weaker, and labelled as such.
- `just real cities` (and `languages`): generate the questions, load, run the
  baselines and the trained cores, score. `just steps` gains a `real` column
  next to test, `val` and `ood`.
- A guard test: no module outside `realtext/` and the scorer reads
  `entities.jsonl`, `questions.jsonl` answers or `redactions.jsonl`.

## What we expect

Low scores at first: 59% (cities) and 49% (languages) of words are handled
with the palier-1 vocabulary, and the rules match seen wordings only. The
point is the trend palier after palier, and whether abstention holds on real
text: a core that never abstains, or always does, shows at once.

## Order of work, once D1–D6 are settled

1. Question generator and its tests (determinism, every shape, answerable
   and unanswerable checked against the texts).
2. Redaction, if D1 keeps it, with its check.
3. Loader and the `real` split (schema PR for Franck).
4. Eval reading `real-v0`, the supported-citation measure, `just real`,
   the `steps` column, the guard test.
5. First scores on both domains, with the hand-checked sample.
