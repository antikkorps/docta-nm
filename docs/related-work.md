# Related work: chains need steps, sufficiency needs a judge

> Written on 2026-11-20, while the Evaluate core reached ~85% on balanced val with sentence-local heads. Citations marked [M] or [S] were not checked against the paper: verify them before quoting.

Every ingredient of Docta-nm has a precedent, but we found no published work that combines them. The ingredients are: a core trained from scratch on CPU with no real-world facts by construction, an explicit state machine with first-class exits, an exact sufficiency label over retrieved excerpts, and template worlds built against abstention shortcuts. On the current bottleneck, two-hop chains stuck near 60%, the literature agrees on three things. First, chains work when a model gets **at least one explicit attention step per hop over sentence-level units**: Memory Network hops, Universal Transformer recurrence, and the three-layer minimum circuit for in-context two-hop. Second, **supervising which sentences are the supporting facts** is the strongest single lever at small data, taking bAbI chain error from 20–60% to 0%. Third, **asking for one hop at a time and letting a controller chain the hops** generalises better than asking for the whole chain at once. Docta-nm's sentence-local heads have close relatives: ETC's structured masks and SG-Net's structure-restricted attention stream. The 74% → ~85% gain matches what mechanistic work predicts about entity binding in small models. No small-data study compares an absolute sentence-index embedding against a sentence mask, so Docta-nm's negative result on the embedding is itself worth recording. On sufficiency judges, all trained judges we found (CRAG, Adaptive-RAG, Self-RAG) start from pretrained models of 0.77B parameters or more. The one exactly matching definition, "sufficient context" (Joren et al., ICLR 2025), relies on a prompted LLM autorater. A from-scratch, CPU-scale multi-hop sufficiency judge with exact labels appears unoccupied. The main risk prior work raises is shortcut learning on template negatives; SQuAD 2.0 measured it directly. A second risk is transfer from template language to natural text, which nobody has tested.

**How to read the citations.** The research notes used several verification marks, and this report keeps them in one legend. **[V]** means authors, year and venue were checked this session against arXiv, ACL Anthology, PMLR, AAAI, NeurIPS or ICLR pages. **[V, venue unconfirmed]** means the paper was fetched but the page did not show the venue; the venue given comes from memory. **[S]** means seen only in a search snippet or listing, not read. **[M]** means from the researchers' prior knowledge, not re-fetched; check it before quoting numbers. A claim with no URL in the notes was left out. Statements labelled *inference* are our reading of the evidence, not findings of the cited authors.

## 1. Chains in small from-scratch models: one attention step per hop, and supervised supports

### What bAbI found

bAbI task 2 (two supporting facts) and task 3 (three supporting facts) are the chain tasks of the template-generated toy suite ([Weston et al., arXiv 1502.05698, ICLR 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05698)) [V, venue unconfirmed]. With 1k training examples, the strongly supervised Memory Network, which was given the supporting facts during training, scored **100% on task 2 but 20% on task 3**. The adaptive-memory variant, which chooses its own number of hops, scored **100% on both**. An LSTM scored 20% on both ([Weston et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.05698)). *Inference:* the number of memory lookups must match the chain length. The detail that the base model used a fixed k = 2 hops is from memory [M].

Without supporting-fact labels, End-to-End Memory Networks (MemN2N) need multiple hops, 10k examples and training tricks. At 1k they still showed **8.3–62% error on task 2 and 21.9–76.9% on task 3**, against 0% for the strongly supervised model. The authors found that "more computational hops give improved performance", and that attention learns to land on the right supporting sentences across hops ([Sukhbaatar et al., arXiv 1503.08895, NeurIPS 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.08895)) [V, venue unconfirmed; the extracted table's columns were garbled, so only the ranges are reliable]. Architecturally, MemN2N encodes **each sentence as one memory slot** and attends over sentences once per hop, which is the closest bAbI-era analogue of Docta-nm's sentence-local heads. Relation Networks passed 18 of 20 tasks at 10k. The two failures were **exactly the two- and three-supporting-fact tasks** ([Santoro et al., arXiv 1706.01427, NeurIPS 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.01427)) [V, venue unconfirmed]. *Inference:* a single-step pairwise module does not chain. Working Memory Networks, which add memory hops before a relational module, show the same ordering at 10k joint training. Task-2/task-3 error is **0.0/0.0 for strongly supervised MemNN, 1.0/6.8 for MemN2N and 81.9/83.1 for an LSTM** ([Pavez et al., arXiv 1805.09354, ACL 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09354)) [V, venue unconfirmed].

Transformers are the most relevant comparison. A plain Transformer failed **10 of 20 tasks at 10k** (single-task) and "always overfits". The **Universal Transformer**, with weights shared across a recurrent depth, failed none at 10k. Its pondering time grew with the number of supporting facts: about **2.3 steps for one fact, 3.1 for two and 3.8 for three**. Its attention starts uniform and sharpens around the supporting facts over steps ([Dehghani et al., arXiv 1807.03819, ICLR 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03819)) [V, venue unconfirmed]. The UT's seed variance is large, though: task 3 error at 1k joint averages **14.3 ± 18.1** across seeds, while the best seed reaches 5.4. Dynamic Memory Networks, DMN+ and Recurrent Entity Networks also iterate attention over sentence facts. Their per-task numbers were not verified, apart from EntNet solving all 20 tasks per task at 10k, as reported by Pavez et al. [M for the rest].

### Successors: depth, step-wise inference and the two regimes

Every model in CLUTRR degrades on test chains longer than the training chains. A graph attention network over the symbolic graph generalises best, and BERT-based text models trail it ([Sinha et al., arXiv 1908.06177, EMNLP 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06177)) [V]. The authors trace the text models' weakness mainly to **parsing unseen narratives**. With the same paraphrases in train and test, text models become competitive with the graph model. ProofWriter (pretrained T5, fine-tuned) shows that **generating a whole proof at once does not extend in depth**: trained on depth ≤ 3, proof accuracy is 93.2% at depth 3 and 13.8% at depth 5. An **iterative one-step model reaches 87.8% at depth 5** ([Tafjord, Dalvi Mishra, Clark, arXiv 2012.13048, Findings of ACL 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13048)) [V]. That paper has no from-scratch comparison. In the from-scratch setting, GPT-2-style models can learn implicit k-hop reasoning in one forward pass, but **training data grows exponentially with k and the layers needed grow linearly with k**. A curriculum reduces the data need but does not remove it ([Yao et al., arXiv 2505.17923, EMNLP 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17923)) [V].

The mechanistic literature separates two regimes, and Docta-nm sits in the easier one. In the **in-context** regime, where the facts are in the prompt, a **three-layer attention-only transformer trained from scratch** learns two-hop chains among four distractor chains. One- and two-layer models fail, three layers with one head each is the minimum, and MLPs are unnecessary. The circuit has three steps. Layer 1 copies each token's parent, so the bridge carries the source. Layer 2 finds the target bridge. Layer 3 finds the end token carrying that bridge. Training shows **a long plateau of uniform guessing among candidate end tokens, then an abrupt jump to 100%** around 800–1,000 steps ([Guo et al., arXiv 2502.13913, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13913)) [V; venue not verified]. In the **parametric** regime, where facts are stored in the weights, composition needs grokking. An 8-layer model reached near-perfect in-distribution accuracy only after about 50× more steps than it took to fit the training set, and out-of-distribution composition stayed at 0 even after 22M steps. The cause is that upper layers would have to store the second-hop facts a second time. Sharing parameters across layers makes OOD composition possible, though slowly. A higher ratio of inferred to atomic facts speeds grokking, while dataset size alone does not ([Wang et al., arXiv 2405.15071, NeurIPS 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15071)) [V]. A recent from-scratch study traces two-hop OOD failure to the same layer mismatch, and reports that recurrent-style circuit reuse substantially improves OOD two-hop ([Zhang et al., arXiv 2608.07261, Findings of EMNLP 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07261)) [V, read at abstract level only]. Fine-tuned LLMs cannot compose two facts learned in separate documents without chain-of-thought. They do above chance when **both facts are in the prompt** ([Balesni, Korbak, Evans, arXiv 2411.16353](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16353)) [V, preprint].

### Supervising the evidence helps; supervising activations does not

Supporting-fact supervision has a large effect in the small, synthetic, from-scratch regime: **0% error versus 8–77% at 1k on bAbI chains** ([Sukhbaatar et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.08895)). The effect is modest with pretrained models on real text. On HotpotQA, a multi-task supporting-sentence classifier adds about **+1.7 EM / +2.1 F1** ([Yang et al., arXiv 1809.09600, EMNLP 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.09600)) [V, venue unconfirmed]. Supervising hidden activations instead failed: logit-lens targets and staged training did not make pretrained LLMs compose latently. The auxiliary loss went down on training data but did not generalise ([Balesni et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16353)). Decomposition also helps and makes models more robust. DecompRC splits bridging questions into single-hop sub-questions, needs only 400 annotations, and scores **70.57 F1 against BERT's 67.08** on HotpotQA distractor. Under swapped distractors it drops to **59.07, while BERT drops to 44.68** ([Min et al., arXiv 1906.02916, ACL 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02916)) [V, venue unconfirmed]. Bridging questions, the shape of Docta-nm's chains, make up 47% of their multi-hop sample. All decomposers found are pretrained. No evidence exists for or against decomposition with tiny from-scratch models.

### What this means for Docta-nm

The 60% on chains is unlikely to be a fundamental limit, because a from-scratch three-layer transformer solves in-context two-hop ([Guo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13913)). *Inference:* a 4-layer model whose heads are half sentence-local may have **too few global attention steps**. It needs to bind within each sentence, find the sentence about X, carry the bridge city, find that city's sentence, and then make the sufficiency judgement. The levers backed by the literature, in rough order of cost, follow.

The first lever is diagnostic. Check whether chain errors spread evenly over the candidate cities or values present in the excerpts. That is Guo's guessing-plateau signature, and it would mean training longer, adding weight decay, or using a curriculum from one distractor upwards. Run several seeds before drawing conclusions, given the UT's seed variance. The second lever is architectural: one or two more layers, or weight-shared recurrent layers as in the UT ([Dehghani et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03819)) and [Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15071). Separately, raising the share of chain examples follows Wang's inferred-to-atomic ratio result, but that result comes from the parametric regime and is untested in context. The third lever fits the project rules best: **decompose chains in the orchestrator**. The state machine already owns the loop, and the templates make the split deterministic. "Population of the city where X lives" becomes "where does X live?" followed by "population of <city>?". This mirrors ProofWriter-Iterative and DecompRC. It turns the chain judgement into two one-hop judgements, which, if they were independent, would land near 0.92² ≈ 85% given ~92% one-hop sufficiency (*inference*, arithmetic only). It also puts the bridge in the trace, where a human can see and correct it. That serves the "super reflection" goal.

**Decision for the project owner: an auxiliary supporting-sentence loss.** The strongest small-data lever in the literature is a training target saying which sentences are the required supports. Worldgen knows them (`required_sources`, `fact_ids`). Used only as a loss target, they would never be visible at inference. Even so, this means training on ground truth that the project rules keep away from models (`fact_ids` and `required_*` are "never exposed to a model"), and the rule does not spell out whether "exposed" covers training targets. The Evaluate label itself is already derived from ground truth, which is a precedent of a kind. This report does not recommend the loss. It flags the choice for Franck: allow ground-truth-derived per-sentence targets as auxiliary training signal, or keep the core to answer-level and sufficiency-level labels only. If allowed, it would also give a per-excerpt "this carries hop 1 / hop 2" signal in the traces.

## 2. Sufficiency judges exist, but none is small and trained from scratch

### SQuAD 2.0 made no-answer detection standard, and warned about template negatives

SQuAD 2.0 added 53,775 crowd-written unanswerable questions, each with a plausible answer span in the paragraph. Its baselines abstain when a no-answer probability passes a threshold tuned on dev ([Rajpurkar, Jia, Liang, arXiv 1806.03822, ACL 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.03822)) [V]. The pre-BERT systems were trained per task without a pretrained language model, but they used GloVe vectors (BiDAF, DocQA [M]) or ELMo, so they are **not strictly from scratch**. They reached 62–75 F1 against 89.5 for humans: DocQA+ELMo 66.3; Read+Verify, which adds an answer verifier, 74.2 ([Hu et al., AAAI 2019](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/4619)) [V]; U-Net 71.7 ([Sun et al., arXiv 1810.06638](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.06638)) [V, preprint]. The control that matters most for Docta-nm is this one. When the unanswerable questions were generated automatically, DocQA+ELMo scored **83.0 F1 on TF-IDF negatives and 89.6 F1 on rule-based negatives, against 67.6 on human-written ones**. Automatically generated negatives are easier to spot ([Rajpurkar et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.03822)).

### LLM-era raters: the right label, the wrong scale

The definition closest to Docta-nm's Evaluate label is **"sufficient context"**. It asks whether the context holds enough to answer, judged independently of any answer ([Joren et al., arXiv 2411.06037, ICLR 2025](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/33dffa2e3d2ab74a783d1a8c292f66d9-Abstract-Conference.html)) [V]. The authors find that proprietary models often answer wrongly instead of abstaining when context is insufficient, and that selective generation guided by the sufficiency signal raises the share of correct answers among responses by 2–10%. Their autorater is a prompted LLM, reported at about 93% accuracy in the authors' slides ([ICLR slides](https://www.iclr.cc/media/iclr-2025/Slides/30092_7awcPDv.pdf)) [S for the 93% figure]. The small trained judges in this family all start from pretrained backbones and mostly judge something narrower. **Self-RAG's** reflection tokens judge relevance per passage (IsRel) and support for an output (IsSup), inside 7B/13B models with a critic trained on GPT-4 labels ([Asai et al., ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/25f7be9694d7b32d5cc670927b8091e1-Abstract-Conference.html)) [V]. **CRAG's** retrieval evaluator is a fine-tuned T5-large (0.77B) that scores each document as Correct, Ambiguous or Incorrect ([Yan et al., arXiv 2401.15884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15884)) [V, venue unconfirmed]. **Adaptive-RAG** routes questions by complexity *before* retrieval with a T5-large classifier ([Jeong et al., NAACL 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.14403v2)) [V]. **FLARE** triggers retrieval from low-probability tokens and has no trained judge ([Jiang et al., arXiv 2305.06983](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.06983)) [V; EMNLP 2023 venue M].

### Selective QA and abstention: small calibrators can work

Kamath, Jia and Liang show that softmax abstention breaks out of domain. A **random forest over 7 features** trained as a calibrator answers 56% of questions at 80% accuracy, against 48% with raw probabilities ([ACL 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.503)) [V]. A small judge can therefore work when its inputs are well chosen, though this one judges a model's errors, not whether evidence is complete. Kadavath et al.'s P(IK) ("I know") rises when relevant source material is in the context ([arXiv 2207.05221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221v1)) [V]. A core with no facts has no P(IK) in that sense, so its abstention is purely about context sufficiency, which makes it cleaner to measure. The reference survey frames abstention from the query, model and human-values perspectives ([Wen et al., TACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.tacl-1.26)) [V]. Its query perspective corresponds to Docta-nm's `ill_posed_question` exit and its model perspective to `not_found`.

### Multi-hop answerability datasets and learned stopping

**MuSiQue-Full** is the closest dataset design to Docta-nm's. Answerable and unanswerable multi-hop questions come in contrast pairs, and the unanswerable member is made by **removing a supporting paragraph**. Performance drops a further ~14 F1 on the paired version ([Trivedi et al., TACL 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.tacl-1.31)) [V]. The underlying "contrastive support sufficiency" transform comes from Trivedi et al. 2020 [M]. **IIRC** tests recognising that a paragraph is incomplete and finding the missing information in linked documents ([Ferguson et al., EMNLP 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.86)) [V]. RuleTaker and ProofWriter add an **Unknown** label under the open-world assumption, over template-generated rule bases ([ProofWriter, arXiv 2012.13048](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.13048)) [V for the label semantics]. Their models are fine-tuned RoBERTa and T5 [M]. Learned stopping for iterative retrieval exists too, always on top of pretrained LLMs. Stop-RAG trains a Q-value head with Q(λ) ([Park et al., arXiv 2510.14337, NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.14337v1)) [S]. FrugalRAG uses an RL-trained termination policy ([arXiv 2507.07634](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.07634)) [S]. A 2026 preprint argues that one early false positive ends a search too soon, so a stopping classifier should be scored by its **first threshold crossing along each trajectory**, not by per-state accuracy ([arXiv 2608.13237](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.13237)) [S; authors not checked].

### What this means for Docta-nm

We found **no published small model trained from scratch to judge whether retrieved evidence suffices for a multi-hop question**. The field moved to pretrained encoders straight after BERT. Docta-nm's advantage is the label itself: required facts ⊆ facts stated in the excerpts read is **exact**, where Joren et al. need an LLM to approximate it. The main threat is the SQuAD 2.0 result. A judge trained on template negatives can learn surface cues, such as a missing entity name or predicate keyword, instead of sufficiency. Docta-nm's existing guards are the right kind: every entity has exactly one unstated fact, and unanswerable questions take every shape. The literature suggests three more. First, **MuSiQue-style contrast twins**: for each chain, emit a twin whose excerpts lack one hop's source, so only the label differs. Second, **plausible-but-insufficient negatives**: excerpts that name the entity and the predicate but not the value, or cover only the first hop. Third, keep scoring on the sealed `ood` split, as Kamath et al. support. Two scoring changes also follow. Report risk-coverage next to accuracy. And score Evaluate **along each trace**: did it stop at the first sufficient point, or too early? That second change fits the project's commitment to measuring the reasoning process. CRAG's three-way output suggests an optional middle class ("partly covered, dig further") that would feed the "dig" step. That is a structural choice for Franck.

## 3. Method without facts: the goal is shared, Docta-nm's construction is not

### Retrieval instead of memory is settled; suppressing fact learning is new

"Fewer parameters plus a datastore beats a bigger closed-book model" is an established result. RETRO matches GPT-3 on the Pile **with 25× fewer parameters** ([Borgeaud et al., ICML 2022](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/borgeaud22a.html)) [V]. Atlas beats a 540B model on Natural Questions with 64 examples and **50× fewer parameters** ([Izacard et al., arXiv 2208.03299](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.03299)) [V; JMLR 2023 venue M]. REALM and kNN-LM belong to the same line ([REALM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08909), [kNN-LM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00172)) [M]. The 2025–2026 work goes further and keeps facts **out of the weights during pretraining**. **LMLM** masks retrieved factual values from the training loss, so the model learns to look facts up rather than memorise them ([Zhao et al., arXiv 2505.15962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15962)) [V, no venue]. **Knowledgeless Language Models** pretrain on corpora with **named entities anonymised**. They report up to 20–25% relative gains in retrieval-grounded settings with imperfect evidence, plus better calibration and **more reliable abstention** ([Cohen et al., arXiv 2607.12831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12831)) [V, preprint]. That is the most direct outside support for Docta-nm's hypothesis. A scaling study from 30M to 3B parameters recommends deliberately partitioning data between what is internalised and what is retrieved ([Singh et al., arXiv 2604.00715](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00715)) [V]. All of these start from real-world text, so real language and common sense still enter the weights. Karpathy's "cognitive core", a few-billion-parameter model that "maximally sacrifices encyclopedic knowledge for capability", states the same goal informally, known only from secondary write-ups ([LessWrong summary](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/qBsj6HswdmP6ahaGB/andrej-karpathy-on-llm-cognitive-deficits)) [S; no primary source].

### Synthetic data is used to study storage versus manipulation, not to build a retrieving core

Physics of Language Models shows on semi-synthetic biographies that knowledge is extractable only if it was augmented during pretraining ([Allen-Zhu & Li, Part 3.1, ICML 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/allen-zhu24a.html)) [V]. Models retrieve stored attributes but **fail to compare or classify them without chain-of-thought** ([Part 3.2, arXiv 2309.14402](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14402)) [V]. That supports Docta-nm's rule that comparison goes to deterministic code. Small models trained on synthetic data have targeted fluency (TinyStories, [arXiv 2305.07759](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07759) [M]) or algorithmic skill (procedural pretraining, [arXiv 2505.22308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22308) [S]), not an evidence-seeking method. Phi-1 relies on LLM-generated data ([arXiv 2306.11644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11644)) [M], the opposite of Docta-nm's no-LLM rule.

### Fixed controller, learned modules: done, but with large models

Pipelines where code fixes the loop and learned modules fill the steps go back to Neural Module Networks ([Andreas et al., CVPR 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.02799)) [M]. The structurally closest prior work is **Faithful Reasoning**. Fine-tuned selection and inference LMs plus a separate learned **halter** that decides when to stop, inside a fixed loop, scored on trace validity ([Creswell & Shanahan, arXiv 2208.14271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14271)) [V]. It builds on Selection-Inference ([ICLR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.09712)) [V]. It uses large pretrained LMs over closed-world rule sets, with no retrieval over sources of varying reliability. DSPy shares the controller/module split with large LMs ([arXiv 2310.03714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03714)) [M]. ReAct and Toolformer are the contrast case, where the model drives the loop ([ReAct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629), [Toolformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04761)) [M].

### Fictional worlds and exact process scoring

Template worlds (bAbI, CLUTRR) and fictional entities used against memorisation (TOFU, GPT-4-generated, [arXiv 2401.06121](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06121) [M]; FictionalQA, [arXiv 2506.05639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05639) [S]) are long-standing tools. The nearest benchmark is **NeoQA**. It has a fictional knowledge base with missing and misleading evidence, and finds that LLMs use "shortcut reasoning instead of abstaining" ([Glockner et al., Findings of ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.616/)) [V]. Its text is probably LLM-generated [unverified], and it evaluates existing LLMs rather than training a core. Citation metrics (ALCE, [arXiv 2305.14627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627) [M]) and step-level process evaluation (ROSCOE, ReCEval, PRM800K [M]) depend on NLI models, human raters or LLM judges, because real-world ground truth is fuzzy.

### What this means for Docta-nm

Docta-nm takes the LMLM/KLLM idea to its end point. With invented names, invented facts and template language, **no fact can be memorised by construction**. As a design consequence rather than a measured result, the knowledge-conflict problem studied by Longpre et al. and Xie et al. ([arXiv 2109.05052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.05052), [arXiv 2305.13300](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13300)) [M] also disappears. Faithful Reasoning's halter and the KLLM abstention result are the two neighbours to cite and set Docta-nm apart from. The cost of the stronger construction is the open risk prior work raises: **no study tests whether a method learned only on template language transfers to natural text**. CLUTRR's finding that text models fail mostly on unseen narratives sharpens that concern.

## 4. Sentence-local heads and held-out phrasings: a known bias in a new place

### Where the head mix sits

Docta-nm's two sentence-local heads plus two global heads per layer are a hard-mask cousin of hierarchical and global-local attention. HAN encodes words within sentences, then sentences ([Yang et al., NAACL 2016](https://aclanthology.org/N16-1174)) [V]. HIBERT does the same with transformers ([Zhang et al., ACL 2019](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1499/)) [V]. **ETC** mixes global and local attention with relative labels for structured input. Its global tokens can stand for sentences, with long tokens masked to their segment ([Ainslie et al., EMNLP 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.19)) [V; the sentence-mask details are from memory]. Longformer and BigBird mix windowed and global attention, and BigBird keeps full attention's expressivity ([Longformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.05150), [BigBird](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2020/hash/c8512d142a2d849725f31a9a7a361ab9-Abstract.html)) [V]. The closest analogue is **SG-Net**, which runs a hard structure-masked attention stream (syntactic dependencies) **next to** ordinary full attention, on top of BERT ([Zhang et al., AAAI 2020](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/6511)) [V]. HotpotQA graph models make the same bet one level up, with sentence nodes and auxiliary supporting-sentence objectives (HGN, [arXiv 1911.03631](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.03631); SAE, [AAAI 2020](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/6441)) [V]. Fixed attention costs little where the structure fits: replacing every learned self-attention head with fixed local Gaussians barely moves BLEU, while a single learned cross-attention head recovers most of what hard-coding cross-attention costs ([You, Sun, Iyyer, ACL 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.687)) [V].

### Why the mask helped and the sentence-index embedding hurt

Large LMs bind attributes to entities through emergent "binding ID" vectors that appear in **sufficiently large** models ([Feng & Steinhardt, ICLR 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/9d1b7fc578c0d2d6431fc26d736ecaf3-Abstract-Conference.html)) [V]. They also use ordering IDs ([Dai et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.967.pdf)) [V]. The positional mechanism gets noisy for middle positions as the entity count grows ([Gur-Arieh et al., arXiv 2510.06182](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06182)) [V, preprint]. *Inference:* a 4-layer, d=128 model may never acquire binding IDs, and the sentence mask supplies "same sentence" as the binding signal by construction. That fits the observation that the gain came mostly from binding a property to the entity named in the same sentence. On index embeddings, Segatron gains from sentence and paragraph index encodings, but at pretraining scale, where the index mostly marks boundaries ([Bai et al., AAAI 2021](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/17485)) [V]. The steadier pattern runs the other way. Hard biases buy sample efficiency ([ConViT, ICML 2021](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/d-ascoli21a.html)) [V]. **Relative beats absolute** for systematic generalisation: relative positions reach 100% on SCAN length, and simple choices raise COGS from 35% to 81% ([Csordás, Irie, Schmidhuber, EMNLP 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.49)) [V]. Design choices strongly drive compositional generalisation ([Ontañón et al., ACL 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.251)) [V; specific findings M]. We found **no controlled small-data comparison of an absolute segment-index embedding against a segment mask**.

### What held-out templates do to accuracy

Models near-perfect in distribution collapse on held-out compositions. SCAN "jump" gets **1.2%**. But with 8, 16 or 32 composed examples in training, accuracy reaches **38%, 78% and 88%**: variety matters more than volume ([Lake & Baroni, ICML 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/lake18a.html)) [V]. On CFQ maximum-compound-divergence splits, all three architectures average **under 20%**, and accuracy falls as compound divergence rises ([Keysers et al., ICLR 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09713)) [V]. COGS finds unseen structures far harder than new words ([Kim & Linzen, EMNLP 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.731)) [V]. Grounded SCAN baselines fail 7 of 8 splits ([Ruis et al., NeurIPS 2020](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/e5a90182cc81e12ab5e72d66e0b46fe3-Abstract.html)) [V]. In QA, held-out question templates cost about **3.5 F1** in zero-shot relation extraction ([Levy et al., arXiv 1706.04115](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04115)) [V]. Much of the apparent KGQA performance came from **template leakage across splits** ([Linjordet & Balog, arXiv 2009.04915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.04915)) [V]. Csordás et al. stress that differences between models are **invisible on the IID split**.

### What this means for Docta-nm

Expect a real `ood` gap. Held-out phrasings are lexical/phrasal novelty, where drops are usually a few points. Held-out chain combinations (`HELD_OUT_CHAINS`) are compound divergence in the CFQ sense, where drops can be severe. Five actions follow from the literature. First, measure `ood` **before and after** the sentence mask: a structural bias that ignores phrasing should help more out of distribution than in it. Second, try a **soft middle ground** between the hard mask and free attention: a learned per-head bias for same versus different sentence, in the style of ETC's relative labels or ConViT's gating. Also run the 3-local/1-global and 1-local/3-global ablations that You et al. motivate. Third, try relative or rotary token positions if positions are currently absolute. Fourth, since `val` uses in-distribution templates, early stopping on it can pick checkpoints that generalise worse. A second validation set drawn from a separate held-out template pool, never from the sealed `ood` templates, avoids that. Fifth, check whether the `ood` templates introduce words missing from the training vocabulary. With word tokens, the split would then partly test tokenisation, which ties into the open tokenisation question. Bucketing validation accuracy by excerpt count would also test the binding prediction: an unmasked model should degrade faster than a masked one as entities multiply.

## Conclusion

The literature changes how to read the 60% plateau: it says more about **missing sequential attention steps** than about model size. Docta-nm already owns the tool that bAbI successors and ProofWriter found most robust, an external loop that can ask one hop at a time. Moving chain decomposition into the orchestrator turns a known weakness of small transformers into a strength of the architecture. It also produces the inspectable intermediate step that the "super reflection" goal calls for. The other levers (more or shared layers, longer training past a guessing plateau, multiple seeds) are cheap to test and well supported. The supporting-sentence loss is the strongest-evidenced lever, but it needs a ruling on what "never exposed" means for training targets.

What Docta-nm can contribute is mostly negative space in the literature: a from-scratch judge of multi-hop sufficiency with exact labels, scored per state along traces, on worlds where memorisation is impossible by construction. That claim holds only if two risks are measured rather than assumed. One is shortcut learning on template negatives; SQuAD 2.0 showed it inflating scores by 15–22 F1. The other is the template-to-natural-text gap that no one has tested. Contrast twins, plausible-but-insufficient negatives and a second held-out-template validation pool address the first. The second is a research question in its own right, and stating it openly strengthens the novelty claim.
